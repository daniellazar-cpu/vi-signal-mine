Not a dashboard and not a page of data: a briefing document with a cover, a numbered figure, captioned tables and an appendix that reads as an appendix. The reference is a pharma commercial review deck or a journal article — authority from typography and restraint, findings quotable straight off the page, every figure traceable to a dated row with a URL. Four rules hold everywhere below. * **Three layers, separated on more than three axes at once.** The answer is the largest type in full ink (the cover figure, the finding claim); the support sits beside it at meta size in secondary ink (the badge, the pips, the volume); the depth moves to a *place* — the appendix, the methodology — rather than to a control. This page carries no disclosure element at all, on purpose and under test: whatever a given print engine does with a closed one cannot matter for the document a client is handed. Enclosure, position and density do the work a summary control does elsewhere. * **Quantity is seen.** The theme volumes are a ranked bar list and the clinician-patient gap is a dumbbell whose connector length *is* the gap. Neither is a wall of digits, and each still prints every number as text — there is no hover layer in this app for a value to hide in. * **`None` is never a blank and never a zero.** Every absent figure states the reason it is absent: the window, the mention count, the two stance columns, the gap column, the citation list. A theme one audience never raised is grouped away from the comparison entirely, because averaging it in would say the silence was agreement. * **Nothing describes a number already on screen.** The prose that survives is the prose that adds something the figures cannot: the bar a statement had to clear, why a theme cannot be compared, what was excluded. --------------------------------------------------------------------------- #}
